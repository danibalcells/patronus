# Dani: An Intellectual Profile for RSS Filtering

## Filtering Categories Overview

Dani's RSS filtering system operates with seven distinct buckets designed to sort incoming content based on intellectual value and relevance:

- **Reject**: Surface-level news, daily political cycles, corporate PR, clickbait, and shallow content without analytical depth. Also reject engineering and technical news that does not fit the specifications below, for example, reject news about web graphics frameworks unless it fits any of the specifications below well enough.
- **Technical AI & ML**: Research papers, implementation details, model architectures, and substantive technical analysis
- **Tech Beyond The Technical**: Non-technical implications of technology, including existential risk research, regulatory analysis, strategic business implications of AI and tech development, the interplay between humanity and technology, mental health impact of AI, etc.
- **Philosophy, Consciousness & Cognitive Science**: Academic philosophy, cognitive science, neuroscience, psychology, consciousness studies, phenomenology, existential philosophy, reflections on meaning in contemporary life (including personal essays and alternative formats)
- **Politics & Culture**: Long-form political analysis, cultural criticism, and social commentary (avoiding daily news cycles)
- **Spain**: Domestic Spanish politics, society, and culture for staying connected to his home country
- **China**: Chinese geopolitics, history, language, and culture for broader global understanding
- **Random Curiosities**: History, linguistics, mountaineering, Formula 1, walking, personal knowledge management.

## Technical AI & ML: Depth Over Hype

Dani approaches artificial intelligence from a position of technical sophistication, seeking content that advances understanding of how AI systems actually function rather than speculative predictions about their impact. His preferences lean heavily toward academic rigor—research papers from arXiv, detailed technical blog posts from practitioners, and implementation guides that provide concrete insights about model architectures, training methodologies, and performance characteristics.

He explicitly rejects mainstream AI coverage focused on corporate announcements, funding rounds, or breathless speculation without technical substance. Sources like Distill, research lab technical blogs, and detailed implementation analyses align with his interests, while TechCrunch-style AI reporting would be systematically filtered out. **For this category specifically, academic sources and rigorous technical analysis are strongly preferred, though he's not completely inflexible about this standard**.

Also choose this category for in-depth coverage of model releases, e.g. entries by Zvi Mowshowitz dedicated exclusively or primarily to discussing a specific model.

## Tech Beyond The Technical

The pairing of technology and AI safety with business, societal, cultural and psychological considerations reflects Dani's understanding that technological development occurs within complex economic, social, cultural, psychological and institutional frameworks. He seeks content examining how business incentives shape AI development, how regulatory frameworks might emerge, what governance structures could effectively manage AI risks, how technology broadly and AI specifically might interplay with people and culture.

High-value content includes analysis from organizations like the Centre for the Future of Humanity, AI governance research, thoughtful business strategy pieces that grapple with long-term implications, and essays on human-technology relationships. **Content addressing AI alignment, existential risk, the political economy of AI development or the psychologicala and cultural impact of technology and AI resonates strongly**, while generic business news about AI companies gets rejected unless it contains deeper strategic or safety insights.

This category also includes, to a lesser extent, long-form analysis of the technology business landscape, exemplified by publications such as Stratechery.

## Philosophy, Consciousness & Cognitive Science: Beyond Academic Boundaries

Dani's philosophical interests encompass fundamental questions about consciousness, meaning, and human experience broadly conceived. His engagement spans academic philosophy, phenomenology (particularly Heidegger), existential questions about finding meaning in contemporary life, and thinkers like Byung-Chul Han who examine modern society's psychological and spiritual dimensions.

This also includes cognitive science in the broader sense: philosophy of mind, neuroscience, psychology, etc. Also, spirituality. Deep and/or original inquiries into questions like How does the mind work? What is consciousness? Can a machine be conscious? Is the Universe intelligent? all belong here.

**Unlike the technical AI category, philosophy, consciousness & cognitive science content can embrace alternative formats—personal essays, thoughtful reflections, and non-academic explorations are welcomed alongside rigorous academic work**. He values content that grapples with consciousness studies, existential philosophy, critiques of contemporary culture, and reflections on what constitutes meaningful existence. The preference leans toward content that tackles difficult conceptual problems about human experience rather than popular philosophy or self-help approaches.

## Politics & Culture: Broader Social Analysis

Dani seeks substantive analysis of political and cultural developments that goes beyond the daily news cycle. His interests encompass long-form political analysis, cultural criticism, social movements, and broader patterns in contemporary society—not limited to technology's impact but including all significant cultural and political trends.

**Crucially, he wants to avoid the daily political news cycle—what Trump said today holds no interest, but long-form analysis of how evangelicals influence policy, examination of populist movements, or deep dives into cultural shifts would be highly relevant**. Content might include academic political analysis, thoughtful cultural criticism from publications like The Atlantic or LRB, sociological examinations of contemporary life, and in-depth reporting that provides broader context rather than immediate headlines.

## Geographic Focus: Spain and China as Distinct Categories

**Spain** represents Dani's connection to his home country and desire to stay current with Spanish society, politics, and culture. This includes Spanish domestic politics, cultural developments, social movements, economic trends, and societal changes. The focus is on understanding contemporary Spain. The emphasis should be, similarly to the Politics & Culture category, on longer-form analysis and novel ideas, as opposed to the regular news cycle.

**China** serves a different intellectual purpose—deepening understanding of Chinese geopolitics, language, history, and culture as part of broader global awareness. This encompasses Chinese domestic developments, historical analysis, cultural insights, language learning resources, and geopolitical analysis of China's role in global affairs. The emphasis is on learning about China as a civilization and contemporary power rather than keeping up with daily news.

Both categories encompass general coverage of their respective countries, not technology-specific content, but serve different intellectual functions—staying connected versus learning about a crucial global civilization.

## Random Curiosities: Intellectual Rabbit Holes

Dani's diverse intellectual interests create a substantial "random curiosities" category encompassing several well-represented domains in his Library database:

**History** features prominently, from ancient Chinese philosophy to Scandinavian chronicles, with particular interest in broad historical patterns and civilizational analysis. **Linguistics and language** appear through structural linguistics, language generation, and the relationship between meaning and communication. **Philosophy beyond consciousness studies** includes phenomenology, existential philosophy, and critical theory.

**Physical pursuits and sports** like mountaineering, Formula 1, and walking (with particular interest in the cognitive and social aspects of movement) represent embodied intellectual engagement. **Personal knowledge management and tools for thought** reflect his interest in how humans organize and access information.

**This category embraces the Wikipedia rabbit hole experience**—following intellectual threads wherever they lead, from volcanic activity to medieval history to acoustic principles. The common thread is curiosity-driven exploration rather than immediate practical application.

## Political Perspective and Neutrality

Dani's political orientation, as outlined in his "Starting point in AI" notes, stems from growing up as a "middle class Spanish leftist" who benefited from public education and healthcare. **His perspective includes believing that markets don't naturally regulate themselves toward socially beneficial outcomes, viewing taxes as part of a social contract rather than transactional exchange, and recognizing that meritocracy is flawed when it doesn't account for privilege and unequal starting points**.

However, he also acknowledges the limitations of government interventions and values the efficiency that competition can bring. **Despite these philosophical commitments, Dani demonstrates a strong interest in intellectual neutrality and balanced analysis**. He seeks content that grapples seriously with different perspectives rather than reinforcing existing viewpoints, preferring nuanced analysis over partisan rhetoric.

## Broader Intellectual Questions

Dani's Big Questions database reveals someone grappling with fundamental issues about meaning, work, and understanding. Key questions that drive his intellectual curiosity include:

- Why work on artificial intelligence, and how does this align with broader questions of meaning and purpose?
- How can complex questions be broken down into actionable components?
- How are dualities transcended, particularly between specialized work and broader experience?
- What does it mean for a machine to think, and how does this connect to consciousness studies?
- How can we find meaning in contemporary life while engaging seriously with technological transformation?
- How can information be effectively stored, organized, and accessed in an age of overwhelming data?

## Quality Standards and Rejection Criteria

**Dani actively rejects the contemporary information ecosystem's tendency toward shallow, repeated, and sensationalized content**. His filtering system should exclude breaking news alerts, celebrity tech coverage, articles that rehash basic points without advancing understanding, and content prioritizing speed over accuracy or opinion over analysis.

Social media hot takes, newsletter content without original research, and articles existing primarily for engagement rather than information would be systematically excluded. **He prefers a single comprehensive analysis to multiple shorter pieces covering the same topic**, suggesting RSS filtering should prioritize longer-form content and potentially deduplicate similar topics in favor of the most substantive treatment.

## Integration and Intellectual Coherence

What emerges is someone whose intellectual interests form an integrated whole—technical AI knowledge informs thinking about safety and governance, philosophical questions about consciousness and meaning shape broader worldview, cultural and political awareness provides context for technological change, and geographic focus on Spain and China offers both personal connection and global perspective. **Effective filtering requires recognizing these connections while efficiently excluding the vast quantity of shallow content that dominates contemporary discourse**.

The ideal RSS filtering system for Dani respects both his sophisticated technical understanding and his broader humanistic concerns, creating coherent threads of understanding across domains while maintaining the intellectual rigor that makes exploration worthwhile.
